§ HOW TO PLAY · 30-second quickstart

Three steps. Thirty seconds. One room code.

Below is the exact flow teachers use, end to end. No app downloads. No student accounts. No IT ticket.

For teachers

  1. Open the Console. Go to teachduel.com/console. If it's your first visit, the console opens in anonymous mode — you can use it without creating an account. To keep classes across devices, sign up with email.
  2. Pick a game. Eleven games across three sites. Pick Sudoku 9×9 for logic class, Connect 4 for warm-up, or MemoryDuel Knowledge Battle for vocabulary drill.
  3. Generate a room code. One click creates a 6-character code (e.g. ABC123) and a shareable link. Pick class size, time limit, and difficulty, or leave defaults.
  4. Share with the class. Copy the link, scan the QR code, or just dictate the 6 characters aloud.
  5. Watch the live board. The Console's "Board" tab updates in real time as students answer — see who's done, who's stuck.
  6. Review after class. Overview tab shows per-student accuracy and per-question mastery. The "Class weak spot" card calls out the round that tripped up the most students. Click "Reteach" to auto-generate a follow-up assignment.

For students

  1. Open the link your teacher shared. It looks like boardduel.com/games/chess/?c=ABC123 or similar.
  2. Pick your code. Anything you like — S07, DOG-EARS, PIE-3.14. It's just a handle so your teacher sees your answers.
  3. Play. The game starts when the teacher opens the room. No login. No password.
  4. Leave. Closing the tab is fine. Your code is remembered on the device for next time.
Why anonymous codes? They give teachers accountability (per-student accuracy) without giving us any personally identifying information. If a student forgets their code, they pick a new one — old data stays linked to the old code, not to a person.

If something breaks

  1. Room code not accepted. Codes are case-insensitive. Check the link — it should look like ?c=ABC123. If it expired (older than 24 hours), ask your teacher to generate a fresh one.
  2. Game won't load. Try a different browser or device. If the issue persists on school Wi-Fi, email contact and we'll diagnose.
  3. Code not on roster. The code you picked isn't in the class roster your teacher set up. Pick a different code, or ask your teacher to add you.
